# 90-Day Learning Plan: Becoming a Better Writer

A structured, practice-first plan. The core loop is simple: **read deliberately, write daily, get feedback, revise**. Each phase builds on the last, and every week ends with a review so you can adjust as you go.

## How to Use This Plan

- **Daily commitment:** 45–60 minutes (30 min writing, 15–30 min reading or studying craft).
- **Weekly rhythm:** 5 practice days, 1 feedback/revision day, 1 rest or free-writing day.
- **Track everything:** keep a single journal or folder with every draft. Progress in writing is only visible in hindsight.
- **Adapt freely:** if a week's focus isn't landing, repeat it. The sequence matters less than the daily habit.

---

## Phase 1 — Foundations (Days 1–30)

**Goal:** build the daily habit and master the sentence and paragraph.

### Week 1 (Days 1–7): Establish the Habit
- Write 300 words every day, on anything. Quantity over quality — the goal is showing up.
- Read 20 minutes daily in a genre you want to write in. Mark sentences you admire.
- **Day 7:** Reread everything you wrote. Note one thing you like and one recurring weakness.

### Week 2 (Days 8–14): The Sentence
- Study: clarity, active voice, cutting filler words (very, really, just, quite).
- Exercise: each day, take one paragraph you've written and cut it by 25% without losing meaning.
- Copywork: hand-copy 10 sentences from a writer you admire and note what makes them work.
- **Craft reading:** *On Writing Well* by William Zinsser (ch. 1–7) or *The Elements of Style*.

### Week 3 (Days 15–21): The Paragraph
- Study: topic sentences, one-idea-per-paragraph, transitions between paragraphs.
- Exercise: write a single 150-word paragraph daily that makes one clear point. Rewrite it once.
- Reverse-outline something you read: write one line per paragraph describing its job.

### Week 4 (Days 22–30): Voice and Rhythm
- Read your work aloud daily — mark every place you stumble and fix it.
- Exercise: write the same short scene or explanation three ways (formal, conversational, minimal).
- Vary sentence length deliberately: follow a long sentence with a short one. Feel the difference.
- **Day 30 milestone:** write a 500-word piece start to finish. Save it — you'll rewrite it on Day 90.

---

## Phase 2 — Structure and Craft (Days 31–60)

**Goal:** move from good sentences to well-shaped pieces, and start getting outside feedback.

### Week 5 (Days 31–37): Structure and Outlines
- Study: beginnings that hook, middles that build, endings that land.
- Exercise: outline three pieces you'd like to write (one paragraph per section). Pick one and draft it.
- Reverse-outline a published essay or chapter you love. Map its structure.

### Week 6 (Days 38–44): Revision as a Skill
- Learn the two-pass method: first pass for structure (what stays, what moves, what dies), second pass for sentences.
- Exercise: take your Week 5 draft and revise it twice with a day of rest in between.
- Rule of thumb: your first draft is you telling yourself the story; revision is telling the reader.

### Week 7 (Days 45–51): Showing, Detail, and Concreteness
- Study: concrete nouns and active verbs beat adjectives and adverbs.
- Exercise: describe a place, person, or process daily in 200 words using only specific, sensory detail — no abstractions.
- Replace every "there is/there are" and weak verb ("is", "has", "gets") in one old draft.

### Week 8 (Days 52–60): Feedback Loop
- Share at least two pieces with real readers: a friend, a writing group, an online community (r/writing, Critique Circle, a workshop, or a colleague for work writing).
- Learn to receive feedback: listen for where readers got confused or bored, not just what they'd change.
- Give feedback on two other people's pieces — critiquing others sharpens your own eye fastest.
- **Day 60 milestone:** publish or share one finished piece somewhere with an audience, however small.

---

## Phase 3 — Depth and Identity (Days 61–90)

**Goal:** apply everything to a sustained project and develop your own voice.

### Week 9 (Days 61–67): Pick a Project
- Choose one meaningful project: a long essay, a short story, a blog series, a work document that matters.
- Break it into daily 300–500 word chunks. Outline first, then draft forward without editing.

### Week 10 (Days 68–74): Draft Through Resistance
- Finish the full rough draft of your project. Do not polish — momentum beats perfection.
- When stuck, write the next sentence badly on purpose. You can't revise a blank page.
- Keep reading 20 minutes daily; note structural moves you can steal for your project.

### Week 11 (Days 75–81): Deep Revision
- Let the draft rest 2 days, then revise using the two-pass method from Week 6.
- Read the entire piece aloud. Cut 10–15% of the total word count.
- Get feedback from at least one reader and do one more revision pass based on it.

### Week 12 (Days 82–90): Ship, Reflect, and Plan Forward
- **Days 82–85:** finalize and publish/share your project.
- **Days 86–88:** rewrite your Day 30 piece from scratch without looking at the original, then compare the two. This is your clearest measure of growth.
- **Days 89–90:** write a one-page reflection: What improved? What's still weak? Then design your next 90 days around the weakness.

## Core Resources

**Books on craft (pick 2–3, don't read them all):**
- *On Writing Well* — William Zinsser (nonfiction, clarity)
- *The Elements of Style* — Strunk & White (fundamentals)
- *On Writing* — Stephen King (habit, voice, storytelling)
- *Bird by Bird* — Anne Lamott (process, first drafts)
- *Several Short Sentences About Writing* — Verlyn Klinkenborg (the sentence)
- *Dreyer's English* — Benjamin Dreyer (style and usage)

**Habits that compound:**
- Read more than you write, and read like a writer — asking *how* it works, not just *what* it says.
- Keep a notebook of sentences, phrases, and ideas you encounter.
- Never edit while drafting. Never publish without editing.

## Weekly Review Template

Every 7 days, answer briefly:
1. Did I write every day? If not, what got in the way?
2. What's one sentence I wrote this week that I'm proud of?
3. What's the most useful thing I learned about my writing?
4. What will I focus on next week?
